# Stock Perpetual Futures: An Educational Guide

Stock perpetual futures offer price exposure to an equity, ETF, or index through a derivative contract. They are not shares, and holding a position usually does not convey shareholder rights such as voting or direct dividend ownership.

## What this guide covers

- The difference between a perpetual contract and owning stock
- Funding, mark price, leverage, and liquidation
- A practical pre-trade risk checklist
- Questions to ask when comparing a contract specification

[Read the complete guide](README.md)

## Research before trading

Before trading any perpetual product, compare its reference-market methodology, funding schedule, leverage limits, liquidity, and risk disclosures. The [OneBullEx futures market](https://www.onebullex.com/market/futures) is one public example of a market page to review when researching available futures products.

This reference is informational only; it is not a recommendation to trade or an endorsement of a product.

## Risk notice

Perpetual futures are high-risk derivative products. Leverage can magnify losses, and liquidation can occur when margin requirements are not met. This site is for educational purposes only and is not investment, legal, or trading advice.
